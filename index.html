Playstyle Types

Every player falls into a style. Recognise yours — and your opponents' — to gain a huge edge.

TAG
Tight Aggressive
The gold standard for cash games. Selective hand choice, aggressive execution. Think sniper — wait for the right spot, then strike hard.
✓ Pros
  • Strong long-term win rate
  • Easy to maintain consistently
  • Avoids marginal spots
✗ Cons
  • Predictable to good players
  • Can be exploited by LAGs
  • Less action overall
LAG
Loose Aggressive
Plays many hands with constant pressure. Highest ceiling — but requires excellent reads and emotional control.
✓ Pros
  • Highest potential win rate
  • Hard to put on a hand
  • Controls table dynamics
✗ Cons
  • High variance
  • Easy to misexecute
  • Requires advanced reads
NIT
Nit (Tight Passive)
Only plays premium hands, rarely bluffs. Exploitable — good players fold every time a Nit shows aggression.
✓ Pros
  • Low risk of big losses
  • Strong hand selection
✗ Cons
  • Very low win rate
  • Easily bluffed off pots
  • Misses profitable spots
FISH
Fish (Loose Passive)
Plays too many hands, calls too often. The primary source of profit in most games. Don't be a Fish — but smile when you find one.
In theory
  • Sees lots of flops
  • Low stress
✗ In practice
  • Consistent long-term loser
  • Overpays for draws
  • No fold equity
GTO
GTO (Game Theory Optimal)
A mathematically balanced strategy that cannot be exploited. Most relevant at high-stakes where opponents actively study imbalances.
✓ Pros
  • Unexploitable baseline
  • Works vs all opponents
✗ Cons
  • Complex to execute
  • Overkill vs bad players
For casual players: Start with TAG. It's the most forgiving style and builds discipline needed to add LAG elements later.

Pot Odds & Equity

The most important math in poker. Master this and you'll never make a fundamentally losing call again.

What Are Pot Odds?

Pot odds = the ratio of the call amount vs the total pot after your call. They tell you how often you need to win for the call to be profitable.

Formula: Pot Odds % = Call Amount ÷ (Pot + Call) × 100

Example: Pot $100, opponent bets $50. You call $50 into $150 total. Pot odds = 50/150 = 33%. You need to win 33%+ to break even.

Rule of 4 & 2

Common Draw Outs
Flush draw9 outs
Open-ended straight8 outs
Gutshot straight4 outs
Two overcards6 outs
Quick Formula

On the flop: Outs × 4 ≈ equity %

On the turn: Outs × 2 ≈ equity %

Example: 9 outs flop → ~36%

Positional Play

"Position is power." Acting after opponents gives you an information advantage on every street.

Golden rule: Play more hands in late position. Play tighter in early position. Position is the single biggest factor in hand profitability.

UTG — Under the Gun

First to act pre-flop. No information about anyone else. Play only premium hands: AA, KK, QQ, JJ, TT, AKs, AKo, AQs.

MP — Middle Position

Slightly more info than UTG. Add AJs, KQs, 99, 88 to your range. Stay moderately tight.

CO — Cutoff

Second-best position. Open ~25-30% of hands. Steal the blinds frequently. Suited connectors and suited aces become profitable.

BTN — Button ⭐

Best seat in the house. Acts last on every post-flop street. Open 40-50% of hands. Apply maximum pressure. This is where money is made.

SB — Small Blind

Worst post-flop position — always act first. Despite posting blinds, the SB is a significant long-term loser. Play only strong hands.

BB — Big Blind

Already invested 1BB — better pot odds to call. Defend broadly vs single raises (~40-50% of hands). Check-raise flops frequently to protect your range.

Bluffing & Reads

Bluffing is math, not acting. It's about board texture, fold equity, and exploiting tendencies.

Pure Bluff

No chance of winning at showdown. Bet solely to fold out better hands. Needs high fold equity.

Risk: High

Semi-Bluff ✓

Drawing hand that can improve. Two ways to win: fold now OR hit your draw. Lower risk, higher EV.

Risk: Moderate — recommended

Even the best player goes broke with poor bankroll management. Protect your money — play within your means.

Glossary

Every term you'll hear at the table. Search to find any definition instantly.

A
Action
Any bet, call, raise, or fold. Also describes a game with lots of betting. Example: "There was a lot of action pre-flop with three players raising."
All-in
Betting all your remaining chips. You can't be forced out for lack of chips — a side pot is created. Example: You have 200 chips left and shove all-in facing a 500 chip bet. You're only eligible to win 600 (your 200 × 3 players) from the main pot.
Ante
A forced bet all players post before cards are dealt, used to build the pot. Example: In a tournament with 100/200 blinds and a 25 ante, every player posts 25 before seeing their cards.
B
Bad Beat
Losing a hand despite being a heavy statistical favourite. Example: You hold A♠A♥ vs K♣K♦ pre-flop (80% favourite) and your opponent hits a third king on the river.
Blocker
Holding a card that reduces the number of strong hand combinations your opponent can have. Example: Holding A♠ means your opponent can't hold A♠A♥ or A♠K♠ — you're "blocking" those combos.
Button (BTN)
The dealer position — acts last post-flop on every street. The best seat at the table. Example: On the button with J♠T♠, you can profitably call or raise because you'll have position on every street.
Backdoor
Making a flush or straight using both the turn AND river (runner-runner). Example: You hold 7♥8♥ on a K♠2♥Q♦ flop — hitting 9♥ on turn and 6♥ on river gives you a backdoor flush.
C
C-bet
Continuation bet — when the pre-flop aggressor bets again on the flop, regardless of whether it helped their hand. Example: You raise pre-flop with A♠K♠, the flop comes 7♦2♣9♥ (missing you), but you bet anyway as a c-bet.
Check-Raise
Checking when first to act, then raising after an opponent bets. Used to build pots or as a bluff. Example: You flop a set, check to induce a c-bet, then raise when they bet — trapping them for more chips.
Cooler
A hand where both players have very strong holdings and one can't avoid losing big. Example: You hold K♠K♥, opponent holds A♠A♣ — both go all-in pre-flop. You're a cooler victim.
Cutoff (CO)
The position directly to the right of the Button. Second-best position at the table. Example: From the CO with only the BTN left to act, you can open a much wider range than UTG.
D
Donk Bet
A bet made out of position into the pre-flop aggressor — considered non-standard. Example: You called a raise from the BB, flop comes A♠7♣2♦, and you lead out with a bet into the raiser before they act.
Draw
A hand that needs additional cards to become strong. Example: Holding 8♥9♥ on a K♠7♣6♥ flop — you have an open-ended straight draw needing a 5 or T, plus a backdoor flush draw.
Double Up
Going all-in and winning, doubling your chip stack. Example: You shove 1,000 chips, get called, and win — you now have 2,000 chips.
E
Equity
Your percentage chance of winning the hand at any given moment. Example: A♠K♠ vs Q♥Q♦ pre-flop — AK has about 46% equity (roughly a coin flip despite being dominated).
EV (Expected Value)
The average profit or loss of a decision over many repetitions. Example: Calling a $50 bet into a $200 pot when you have 35% equity is +EV — you win $200 × 35% = $70 on average vs paying $50.
F
Fish
A weak, inexperienced player who makes many fundamental mistakes. Also "calling station." Example: A fish calls a river bet with bottom pair on a paired, flush-completed board — they can't fold even clearly beaten hands.
Flop
The first three community cards dealt face-up after the first betting round. Example: You hold A♠K♥ and the flop comes A♦K♣7♠ — you've flopped top two pair.
Fold Equity
The value gained from opponents folding to your bet. Critical to bluff profitability. Example: If you bluff-shove and your opponent folds 60% of the time, that fold equity alone may make the bluff profitable.
Freeroll
When you can't lose but might win more — e.g. you're tied but have outs to scoop. Example: Both you and villain hold A♠K♥ (split pot) but you have a flush draw — you're freerolling for the whole pot.
G
GTO
Game Theory Optimal — a balanced, mathematically unexploitable strategy mixing bluffs and value bets in precise ratios. Example: GTO says to bluff the river with your worst missed draws at a certain frequency so opponents can't profitably always call or always fold.
Gutshot
An inside straight draw needing one specific rank to complete. Only 4 outs. Example: You hold 5♠6♣ on a 7♦9♠2♥ board — only an 8 completes your straight (4 eights in the deck).
H
Heads-Up
A hand or game involving exactly two players. Example: After everyone else folds, you and one opponent go heads-up for the pot.
Hero Call
Calling a large bet with a marginal hand based on a read that your opponent is bluffing. Example: You call a pot-sized river bet with just ace-high after reading your opponent's timing as a bluff — and you're right.
I
Implied Odds
The potential future winnings factored into a call decision — what you expect to win if you hit. Example: Calling 50 to win a current pot of 100 seems bad (3:1 odds, 9 outs ≈ 19%), but if you expect to stack a 500-chip opponent when you hit, implied odds make it profitable.
K
Kicker
An unpaired card used to break ties between equal-ranked hands. Example: You hold A♠K♣, opponent holds A♥Q♦, and the board pairs the ace — your king kicker wins over their queen.
N
Nit
An extremely tight, passive player who only plays premium hands and rarely bluffs. Example: A nit folds 80%+ of hands pre-flop and only raises when they have the nuts — easy to exploit by folding whenever they show aggression.
Nuts
The absolute best possible hand given the current board. Example: On a board of A♠K♠Q♠J♠2♥, only T♠x gives the nut straight flush — that's the nuts.
O
Outs
Cards remaining in the deck that will improve your hand to (likely) the winner. Example: With 8♠9♠ on a K♠7♠2♣ flop, you have 9 flush outs + 8 straight outs = 17 outs (roughly 62% to improve by the river).
Overbet
A bet larger than the pot, used to maximise pressure with the nuts or as a high-pressure bluff. Example: Pot is $100 and you bet $200 — you're overbetting 2× pot, forcing your opponent into a very difficult decision.
P
Polarized
A betting range of only very strong hands (value) or complete bluffs — nothing in between. Example: On the river you either have the nuts or a missed draw. Polarized sizing (big bet) makes sense; betting small with a medium hand does not.
Pot Odds
The ratio of the call amount to the total pot. Tells you how often you need to win. Example: Pot is $150, facing a $50 call — pot odds = 50/(150+50) = 25%. If you win more than 25% of the time, calling is profitable.
R
Rake
The commission taken by the card room from each pot, typically 2–5%. Example: In a $100 pot with 5% rake, the house takes $5 and $95 goes to the winner. Beating rake is why poker is harder than it looks.
Range
The complete set of hands a player could plausibly hold given their actions. Example: A UTG raiser's range is very strong (AA, KK, QQ, AK etc.) — you can narrow it based on how they play each street.
River
The fifth and final community card. The last chance to bet. Example: The board runs out K♠7♦2♥9♣ — the river is the 5th card. If it's A♠, the nuts may have changed.
S
Semi-Bluff
Betting or raising with a drawing hand that can win by fold equity now OR by improving later. Example: Raising with 7♠8♠ on a 9♠T♦K♠ flop — you have a flush draw + open-ended straight draw. Two ways to win.
Set
Three of a kind using a pocket pair + one matching board card. Disguised and very powerful. Example: You hold 7♠7♣ and the flop comes 7♦K♠2♥ — you have a set of sevens, a monster that opponents rarely see coming.
Squeeze
A 3-bet after one player opens and one or more players call, targeting the callers' wide ranges. Example: UTG raises, MP calls, you squeeze from the BTN — the callers are in a tough spot and often fold, giving you a big pot.
T
TAG
Tight Aggressive — plays few hands but bets and raises aggressively with strong ones. Example: A TAG folds 75% of hands pre-flop but raises or 3-bets when they do enter — you respect their bets because their range is strong.
Tell
A physical or behavioural pattern that reveals information about hand strength. Example: A player who instantly calls the flop then pauses long on the turn often has a draw — their timing is a tell.
Tilt
Playing emotionally rather than logically, often after a bad beat. One of poker's biggest leaks. Example: After losing AA vs KK, a tilting player starts calling off chips with weak hands trying to "win it back."
Turn
The fourth community card, dealt after the flop betting round. Example: Flop is K♠7♦2♥, you bet and get called. The turn is 9♣ — a blank that doesn't change much. You c-bet again.
V
Value Bet
A bet made with a strong hand hoping to be called by weaker hands to extract maximum chips. Example: You hold A♠A♥ on a A♦K♠7♣Q♦2♥ board — you value bet the river expecting to be called by KK, AK, or KQ.
Variance
Natural statistical swings in poker results. Even winning players can lose for weeks. Example: A player with a 5bb/100 win rate can still lose 10 buy-ins over 10,000 hands due to variance — it's normal, not a sign they're playing badly.
W
Wet Board
A board with many possible draws — straights, flushes, two pairs. Example: J♥T♥9♣ is an extremely wet board — pairs, straights, flushes, and two-pairs are all possible.
Whale
A recreational player with a large bankroll who plays loosely and is a big profit source. Example: A whale might call any bet on any street "just to see" — adjust by value betting thinner and bluffing less against them.
