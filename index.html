Playstyle Types

Every player falls into a style. Recognise yours — and your opponents' — to gain a huge edge.

TAG
Tight Aggressive
The gold standard for cash games. Selective hand choice, aggressive execution. Think sniper — wait for the right spot, then strike hard.
✓ Pros
  • Strong long-term win rate
  • Easy to maintain consistently
  • Avoids marginal spots
✗ Cons
  • Predictable to good players
  • Can be exploited by LAGs
  • Less action overall
LAG
Loose Aggressive
Plays many hands with constant pressure. Highest ceiling — but requires excellent reads and emotional control. The best players in the world tend to be LAGs.
✓ Pros
  • Highest potential win rate
  • Hard to put on a hand
  • Controls table dynamics
✗ Cons
  • High variance
  • Easy to misexecute
  • Requires advanced reads
NIT
Nit (Tight Passive)
Only plays premium hands, rarely bluffs. Exploitable — good players fold every time a Nit shows aggression, giving away the hand type.
✓ Pros
  • Low risk of big losses
  • Strong hand selection
✗ Cons
  • Very low win rate
  • Easily bluffed off pots
  • Misses profitable spots
FISH
Fish (Loose Passive)
Plays too many hands, calls too often. The primary source of profit in most games. Don't be a Fish — but smile when you find one.
In theory
  • Sees lots of flops
  • Low stress
✗ In practice
  • Consistent long-term loser
  • Overpays for draws
  • No fold equity
GTO
GTO (Game Theory Optimal)
A mathematically balanced strategy that cannot be exploited. Relevant at high-stakes or tournament play where opponents actively study imbalances.
✓ Pros
  • Unexploitable baseline
  • Works vs all opponents
✗ Cons
  • Complex to execute
  • Overkill vs bad players
For casual players: Start with TAG. It's the most forgiving style, produces consistent results, and builds the discipline needed to add LAG elements later.

Pot Odds & Equity

The most important math in poker. Master this and you'll never make a fundamentally losing call again.

What Are Pot Odds?

Pot odds = the ratio of the call amount vs the total pot after your call. They tell you how often you need to win for the call to be profitable.

Formula: Pot Odds % = Call Amount ÷ (Pot + Call) × 100

Example: Pot $100, opponent bets $50. You call $50 into $150 total. Pot odds = 50/150 = 33%. You need to win 33%+ of the time to break even.

Rule of 4 & 2

Common Draw Outs
Flush draw9 outs
Open-ended straight8 outs
Gutshot straight4 outs
Two overcards6 outs
Quick Formula

On the flop: Outs × 4 ≈ equity %

On the turn: Outs × 2 ≈ equity %

Example: 9 outs flop → ~36%

Positional Play

"Position is power." Acting after opponents gives you an information advantage on every street.

Golden rule: Play more hands in late position. Play tighter in early position. Position is the single biggest factor in hand profitability.

UTG — Under the Gun

First to act pre-flop. No information about anyone else. Play only premium hands: AA, KK, QQ, JJ, TT, AKs, AKo, AQs.

MP — Middle Position

Slightly more info than UTG. Add AJs, KQs, 99, 88 to your range. Stay moderately tight.

CO — Cutoff

Second-best position. Open ~25-30% of hands. Steal the blinds frequently. Suited connectors and suited aces become profitable.

BTN — Button ⭐

Best seat in the house. Acts last on every post-flop street. Open 40-50% of hands. Apply maximum pressure. This is where money is made.

SB — Small Blind

Worst post-flop position — always act first. Despite posting blinds, the SB is a significant long-term loser. Play only strong hands.

BB — Big Blind

Already invested 1BB — better pot odds to call. Defend broadly vs single raises (~40-50% of hands). Check-raise flops frequently to protect your range.

Bluffing & Reads

Bluffing is math, not acting. It's about board texture, fold equity, and exploiting tendencies.

Pure Bluff

No chance of winning at showdown. Bet solely to fold out better hands. Needs high fold equity.

Risk: High

Semi-Bluff ✓

Drawing hand that can improve. Two ways to win: fold now OR hit your draw. Lower risk, higher EV.

Risk: Moderate — recommended

Bankroll Management

Even the best player goes broke with poor bankroll management. Protect your money — play within your means.

Why BRM matters: Poker has variance. Even good players lose for weeks. Proper BRM ensures you survive downswings and let your edge materialise.

Glossary

Every term you'll hear at the table. Search to find any definition instantly.

A
Action
Any bet, call, raise, or fold.
All-in
Betting all remaining chips.
Ante
A forced bet all players post before each hand.
B
Bad Beat
Losing despite being a strong statistical favourite.
Blocker
Holding a card that reduces the combinations of strong hands your opponent can have.
Button (BTN)
The dealer position — acts last on every post-flop street.
C
C-bet
Continuation bet — when the pre-flop aggressor bets again on the flop.
Check-Raise
Checking then raising after an opponent bets. Powerful aggressive move.
Cooler
Both players have very strong holdings — e.g. AA vs KK. Hard to avoid losing.
D
Donk Bet
A bet out of position into the pre-flop aggressor. Non-standard.
Draw
A hand needing additional cards to become strong (e.g. four cards to a flush).
E
Equity
Your % share of the pot based on probability of winning.
EV (Expected Value)
Average profit or loss of a decision over many repetitions.
F
Fish
A weak, inexperienced player. Also "calling station".
Flop
The first three community cards dealt face-up.
Fold Equity
Value gained from opponents folding. Key to bluff profitability.
G
GTO
Game Theory Optimal — a balanced, unexploitable strategy.
Gutshot
An inside straight draw needing one specific rank. Only 4 outs.
H
Heads-Up
A hand involving exactly two players.
Hero Call
Calling a large bet with a weak hand based on a read that your opponent is bluffing.
I
Implied Odds
Potential future winnings factored into a call. Makes some draws profitable.
N
Nit
An extremely tight, passive player who only plays premium hands.
Nuts
The absolute best possible hand given the current board.
P
Polarized
A betting range of only very strong hands or complete bluffs.
Pot Odds
Ratio of pot size to call cost. Used to evaluate call profitability.
R
Rake
Commission taken by the card room from each pot (typically 2-5%).
Range
The complete set of hands a player could have given their actions.
River
The fifth and final community card.
S
Semi-Bluff
Betting with a drawing hand that can win if called or if opponents fold.
Set
Three of a kind using a pocket pair + one board card. Disguised and powerful.
T
TAG
Tight Aggressive — plays few hands but bets and raises aggressively.
Tell
A pattern revealing information about hand strength.
Tilt
Playing emotionally rather than logically. A major leak.
Turn
The fourth community card, after the flop betting round.
V
Value Bet
A bet with a strong hand, hoping to be called by weaker hands.
Variance
Natural statistical swings. Even great players can lose for weeks.
