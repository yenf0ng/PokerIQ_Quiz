Playstyle Types

Every poker player falls into a style. Recognising your own — and your opponents' — is one of the fastest ways to improve your win rate.

TAG
Tight Aggressive
The gold-standard style for most cash games. TAG players are selective about which hands they enter with (tight), but once in, they bet and raise confidently (aggressive). Think of it as a sniper: wait for the right moment, then strike with force.
✓ Pros
  • Strong long-term win rate
  • Easy to execute consistently
  • Avoids marginal spots
  • Respected at the table
✗ Cons
  • Predictable to good players
  • Folds too much to blind pressure
  • Can be exploited by LAGs
  • Less fun / social
LAG
Loose Aggressive
LAGs play many hands and apply constant pressure with bets and raises. This style is extremely profitable at high levels but requires excellent hand-reading skills and strong emotional control. The best poker players in the world tend to be LAGs.
✓ Pros
  • Highest ceiling win rate
  • Hard to put on a hand
  • Controls table dynamics
  • More pots contested
✗ Cons
  • High variance swings
  • Requires advanced reads
  • Easy to misexecute
  • Tilt-prone
NIT
Nit (Tight Passive)
Nits play only premium hands and rarely bet aggressively. They sit and wait for the absolute nuts before committing chips — which is exactly why they're exploitable. Observant players will fold every time a Nit shows aggression, costing them value.
✓ Pros
  • Low risk of big losses
  • Easy to maintain discipline
  • Strong hand selection
✗ Cons
  • Very low win rate
  • Misses profitable spots
  • Easily exploited by bluffs
  • Boring to play against
FISH
Fish (Loose Passive)
Also called a "calling station", Fish play too many hands and call too often without raising. They often see showdowns with weak holdings. The good news? Fish are the primary source of profit in most games. Don't be a Fish — but be happy when you find one.
✓ From a study angle
  • Very low stress
  • See lots of flops
  • Learn by experience
✗ In practice
  • Consistent loser long-term
  • Overpays for draws
  • No fold equity
  • Bleeds chips slowly
GTO
GTO (Game Theory Optimal)
GTO is not a "style" in the traditional sense — it's a mathematically balanced strategy that cannot be exploited. GTO players mix bluffs and value bets in precise ratios. It's most relevant in high-stakes or tournament play where opponents actively try to exploit imbalances.
✓ Pros
  • Unexploitable baseline
  • Works vs all opponents
  • Strong in tough lineups
  • Built on solid theory
✗ Cons
  • Complex to execute
  • Less exploitative EV
  • Overkill vs bad players
  • Solver-dependent study
For casual players: Start with TAG. It's the most forgiving style, produces consistent results, and builds the discipline needed to eventually add LAG elements to your game.

Pot Odds & Equity

The single most important mathematical concept in poker. Master pot odds and you'll never make a fundamentally losing call again.

What Are Pot Odds?

Pot odds are the ratio of the amount you must call versus the total pot after your call. They tell you how often you need to win the hand for the call to be profitable.

Formula: Pot Odds % = Call Amount ÷ (Pot + Call Amount) × 100

Example: Pot is $100, opponent bets $50. You call $50 into a $150 total pot. Pot odds = 50 ÷ 150 = 33%. You need to win at least 33% of the time for the call to break even.

What Are Outs?

Outs are cards remaining in the deck that will improve your hand to (likely) the winner.

Common Draw Outs
Flush draw9 outs
Open-ended straight8 outs
Gutshot straight4 outs
Two overcards6 outs
One overcard3 outs
Set → full house7 outs
Rule of 4 & 2

Quick equity estimation:

On the flop (2 cards to come): Outs × 4 ≈ equity %

On the turn (1 card to come): Outs × 2 ≈ equity %

Example: 9 outs on the flop → 9×4 = ~36% equity

Positional Play

"Position is power." Acting after your opponents gives you an information advantage on every street. Learn to leverage it.

The golden rule: Play more hands in late position. Play fewer, stronger hands in early position. Position is the single biggest factor in your profitability on any given hand.

UTG — Under the Gun

First to act pre-flop and most street post-flop (if players remain). UTG is the most disadvantaged position at the table. You have no information about what anyone else wants to do.

Strategy tip: Open only premium hands from UTG: AA, KK, QQ, JJ, TT, AKs, AKo, AQs. Your range should be tight and strong, as you'll be out of position on most post-flop streets.

MP — Middle Position

Middle position players have slightly more info than UTG (fewer players acted before them) but are still largely out of position post-flop. A moderately tight range is appropriate here.

Strategy tip: Add AJs, KQs, 99, 88 to your UTG range. You can start semi-bluffing suited connectors occasionally, but stay disciplined.

CO — Cutoff

The Cutoff is second only to the Button. You're in position on everyone except the Button. Stealing the blinds from CO is extremely profitable, and you can open a wide range of hands.

Strategy tip: Open around 25-30% of hands. Suited connectors, suited aces, and any pair become profitable here. Raise to steal frequently when the Button has been folding.

BTN — Button

The best seat in the house. The Button acts last post-flop on every street. This is where the most money is made in poker. You can play the widest range, apply maximum pressure, and make the most accurate decisions.

Strategy tip: Open 40-50% of hands from the Button. Three-bet lighter. Float more. You have the maximum informational advantage — use it aggressively.

SB — Small Blind

The Small Blind is the worst post-flop position — you always act first. Despite paying in blind money, the SB is a significant long-term loser for most players. Avoid playing marginal hands from here.

Strategy tip: Complete or raise with strong hands only. Avoid limping speculative hands. When you 3-bet, do so for value or as a bluff with blockers — not with medium-strength hands.

BB — Big Blind

The Big Blind has a unique advantage: you get a discount to see the flop. You've already invested one big blind, so your pot odds to call opens are better than anyone else. Defend your big blind aggressively.

Strategy tip: Defend broadly vs single raises (call with ~40-50% of hands). You have the worst post-flop position, but your pot-odds discount compensates. Check-raise the flop frequently to protect your range.

Bluffing & Reads

Bluffing is not about deception — it's about mathematics, board texture, and exploiting your opponent's tendencies.

Pure Bluff

You have little to no chance of winning at showdown. You bet solely to fold out better hands. High risk, needs high fold equity to be profitable.

Risk level: High

Semi-Bluff

You have a drawing hand that can become the best hand. Two ways to win: they fold now, OR you hit your draw. Lower risk, higher long-term EV.

Risk level: Moderate — recommended

When to Bluff

  • ✓
    You have the best board: Scary boards with aces, high cards, or possible straights/flushes that hit your pre-flop raising range are prime bluffing territory.
  • ✓
    Opponent shows weakness: Check-call patterns or passive play indicates a medium-strength hand that can be pressured off the pot.
  • ✓
    You have position: Bluffing in position is far more effective — you see their action before committing chips.
  • ✓
    You have blockers: Holding a card that blocks your opponent's strong hands reduces the combinations they can have (e.g., holding an ace reduces the number of AA/AK combos they hold).
  • ✗
    Don't bluff calling stations: If your opponent calls everything, bluffing is -EV. Value bet thin instead.

Bankroll Management

The best player at the table can still go broke with poor bankroll management. Protect your money, play within your means.

Why BRM matters: Poker has variance — even good players lose for extended stretches. Proper bankroll management ensures you can survive these downswings and stay in the game long enough for your edge to materialise.

The Rules

  1. Never play with money you can't afford to lose. Poker money should be a separate bankroll, not rent money.
  2. Have a stop-loss rule. If you lose 3 buy-ins in a session, stop. Come back tomorrow with a clear head.
  3. Move down without ego. If your bankroll drops below 15 buy-ins for your stake, drop to a lower level.
  4. Move up carefully. Only shot-take higher stakes when you have 5+ buy-ins for that level as a "shot fund" separate from your main roll.
  5. Track your results. Keep records of sessions (hours, stakes, profit/loss). You can't manage what you don't measure.
  6. Separate recreation from profit. Casual home games? Different money. If you're trying to win, treat it like a business.

Poker Glossary

Every term you'll hear at the table. Search to find any definition instantly.

A
Action
Any bet, call, raise, or fold. Also refers to a game with a lot of betting and raising.
All-in
Betting all remaining chips. A player cannot be forced out of a hand for lack of chips — they play for a side pot.
Ante
A forced bet that all players post before each hand, used to increase pot size and encourage action.
B
Bad Beat
Losing a hand despite being a strong statistical favourite — e.g. AA vs KK pre-flop, and KK wins.
Backdoor
Making a flush or straight using both the turn and river cards (also called "runner-runner").
Big Blind (BB)
The forced bet posted by the player two seats left of the dealer button. Sets the minimum bet size.
Blank
A card that doesn't meaningfully change the board or any player's hand strength.
Blocker
Holding a card that reduces the combinations of strong hands your opponent can have.
Board
The five community cards dealt face-up in Hold'em (flop, turn, river).
Button (BTN)
The dealer position — the best post-flop seat because it acts last on every street.
C
Check
Declining to bet while retaining the right to call or raise later. Only available when no bet has been made.
Check-Raise
Checking, then raising after an opponent bets. A powerful aggressive move to build the pot or as a bluff.
Continuation Bet (c-bet)
A bet made on the flop by the player who was the last pre-flop aggressor, regardless of how the flop hit them.
Cooler
A hand where both players have very strong holdings — e.g. AA vs KK. Hard to avoid losing big.
Cutoff (CO)
The position immediately to the right of the Button. Second-best position at the table.
D
Dead Money
Chips in the pot contributed by players who have already folded. Valuable to contest for.
Donk Bet
A bet made out of position into the pre-flop aggressor. Considered non-standard in most spots.
Double Up
Going all-in and winning, thereby doubling your chip stack.
Draw
A hand that is not yet complete but needs additional cards to become strong — e.g. four cards to a flush.
E
Equity
Your percentage share of the pot based on your probability of winning at showdown given current cards.
Expected Value (EV)
The average profit or loss of a decision over many repetitions. +EV means profitable long-term.
F
Fish
A weak, inexperienced player. Also called a "donkey" or "calling station."
Flop
The first three community cards dealt face-up after the first betting round.
Fold Equity
The value gained from opponents folding to your bet. A key component of bluff profitability.
Freeroll
A tournament with no entry fee. Also: when you can't lose but might win — e.g. a split pot situation where you have outs to scoop.
G
GTO
Game Theory Optimal. A mathematically balanced strategy that is unexploitable by opponents.
Gutshot
An inside straight draw needing one specific rank to complete — e.g. holding 5-6 with 8-9-2 on the board needing a 7. Only 4 outs.
H
Heads-Up (HU)
A hand or game involving exactly two players.
Hero Call
Calling a large bet with a relatively weak hand based on a read that your opponent is bluffing.
Hole Cards
The two private cards dealt face-down to each player at the start of a hand.
I
Implied Odds
The potential future winnings factored into a call decision. If you hit your draw, you expect to win more chips on future streets.
In Position (IP)
Acting after your opponent on a given street — a significant strategic advantage.
K
Kicker
An unpaired card that is used to break ties between hands of the same rank — e.g. AK beats AQ when both pair the ace.
L
LAG
Loose Aggressive. A player who enters many pots and bets/raises aggressively. High skill ceiling.
Limp
Calling the big blind pre-flop rather than raising. Generally considered weak play in most positions.
M
Muck
To discard your hand face-down without showing it. You can muck a losing hand at showdown.
MTT
Multi-Table Tournament. A tournament with multiple tables that gradually consolidate as players are eliminated.
N
Nit
An extremely tight, passive player who only plays the very best hands and rarely bluffs.
Nuts
The absolute best possible hand given the current board. "I had the nuts" = unbeatable hand.
O
Outs
Cards remaining in the deck that will improve your hand to (likely) the best hand.
Overbet
A bet larger than the size of the pot, used to maximize pressure with the nuts or as a high-pressure bluff.
Overpair
A pocket pair higher than any card on the board — e.g. KK on a Q-7-3 board.
P
Polarized
A betting range consisting of only very strong hands (nuts) or complete bluffs — nothing in between.
Pot Odds
The ratio of the pot size to the cost of a call, expressed as a percentage. Used to evaluate call profitability.
Probe Bet
A small bet made out of position when the in-position player checks back the flop, to gain information.
R
Rake
The commission fee taken by the casino or poker room from each pot, typically 2-5%.
Range
The complete set of hands a player could plausibly have in a given situation based on their actions.
River
The fifth and final community card dealt in Hold'em. The last opportunity to bet.
Runner-Runner
Hitting the turn and river to complete a drawing hand — also called backdoor.
S
Semi-Bluff
Betting or raising with a drawing hand that can win if called (by completing) or if opponents fold.
Set
Three of a kind made with a pocket pair + one card on the board. Disguised and very powerful.
Showdown
Revealing hole cards at the end of a hand to determine the winner when two or more players remain.
Small Blind (SB)
The forced bet posted by the player immediately left of the dealer button — half the big blind.
Squeeze
A 3-bet made after one player has opened and one or more players have called, targeting the callers' wide ranges.
T
TAG
Tight Aggressive. Plays few hands but bets and raises aggressively with strong holdings.
Tell
A physical or behavioural pattern that reveals information about a player's hand strength.
Tilt
Playing emotionally rather than logically, often after a bad beat. One of the biggest leaks in poker.
Turn
The fourth community card dealt in Hold'em, after the flop betting round.
U
Under the Gun (UTG)
The player who acts first pre-flop — the most disadvantaged position at the table.
V
Value Bet
A bet made with a strong hand, hoping to be called by weaker hands to extract maximum chips.
Variance
The natural statistical swings in poker results. Even great players can lose for weeks — variance is unavoidable.
W
Whale
A recreational player with a large bankroll who plays loosely. Very profitable to have at your table.
Wet Board
A board texture with many possible draws — e.g. J♥-T♥-9♣. Many hands connect with wet boards.
